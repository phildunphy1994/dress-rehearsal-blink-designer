# Hearth-core living room with a south casement band

Brief: iteration_i1 — Arm: learned. Living room 6.00 m × 4.50 m (interior clear), ceiling 2.40 m. Geometry per the spec convention: x east (length), y north (width), origin at the interior south-west corner.

## Layout (in words)

The room is read as the living half of a long east–west bar. Its short west wall is treated as the central masonry chimney-and-stair core of the house, so the hearth sits in the core on the room's long axis (y 2.25) rather than on an outside wall. The chimney breast projects 0.45 m into the room with a plain brick opening raised on a cast-stone plinth.

The entry door comes from the stair hall beside the chimney: a flush oak leaf in the west wall north of the breast. It opens into a 0.93 m wide entry gallery along the north wall, held under an oak-boarded soffit dropped to 2.10 m, and separated from the seating by a 2.25 m long oak post-and-slat screen that runs east from beside the hearth. The gallery turns south through a 0.95 m channel between the screen's end and the settee, and only then does the room open to its full 2.40 m height and to the fire. The door centre is 1.58 m from the nearest seat (Armchair A).

Seating is gathered on four sides of a square in front of the hearth: two armchairs backing onto the screen (north), a two-seat settee facing the fire (east), and a built-in window seat under the casement band (south); the hearth is the fourth side. A low stone-topped table sits at the centre on a flatweave rug. Six seats in all.

The entire south wall between the chimney end and the east corner is a continuous band of four tall narrow leaded casements between three brick piers, 4.50 m long (75% of the wall), on a 0.45 m sill course with a continuous stepped head band at 2.25 m. A two-leaf pair on the same module lights the east end, with a low oak cabinet beneath it; a cast-stone planter trough continues the sill-wall base east of the window seat.

## Materials and finishes

- Walls: tawny clay brick with only the horizontal coursing expressed (raked bed joints, flush perpends); no vertical joints read. The west wall is the hatched masonry core; north wall is the blank party wall to the service wing.
- Bands and slabs: honed cast stone for the hearth plinth (two stacked slabs), shelf slab, continuous sill course at 0.45 m on the south and east walls, the 0.15 m cap band under the ceiling, the planter trough, cabinet and table tops.
- Floor: quarter-sawn oak boards, 140 mm wide, hardwax-oiled matt.
- Joinery: solid oak, oiled; profiles built from stacked rectangular layers (stepped cap finials, stepped moulded feet, stepped head band with coved soffit); dowelled construction; no carving, turning or applied ornament.
- Glazing: straight-line leaded lattice in every casement — tall narrow border lights and a diaper of shallow lozenges about three times as wide as high — in thin oak frames with plain mullions.
- Upholstery: oxblood leather over stuffing on seat cushions; oatmeal stippled wool on the armchair backs; oatmeal wool flatweave rug.
- Fireplace: plain brick opening lined in red clay firebrick; no surround or mantel.
- Ceiling: limewashed lime plaster; oak-boarded soffit over the gallery with a limewashed fascia.
- Not used anywhere: mirrored wall panels; high-gloss lacquer on any wall surface.

## Decision log

1. Layout — hearth on the west wall treated as the central chimney-and-stair core on the long axis (y 2.25), seating square in front, 0.93 m gallery along the north wall, glazed band on the south. Rationale: hearth kept in a central core rather than on an outside wall, with the room flowing past the core; long low horizontal organisation. Rules: R01, R03.
2. Entry sequence — 0.85 m flush oak door in the west core wall north of the breast, gallery under a 2.10 m soffit, 2.25 m oak screen between door and seats, door centre 1.58 m from the nearest seat, swing kept clear. Rationale: main floor reached from the stair beside the chimney; unexpressed flush door with no surround or step; the oak screen beside the fireplace is the corpus's own device. Rules: R03, R12, R14.
3. Window pattern — 4.50 m band (75%) of four 0.84 m leaded casements between three 0.38 m brick piers on a 1.22 m module, sill course 0.45, head 2.25; two-leaf pair on the east wall. Rationale: continuous ribbon of tall narrow casements between narrow piers with continuous head and sill on the 2'-9" / 1'-3" module; south wall almost entirely glazed above a low wall; straight-line leaded lattice. Rules: R07, R09, R11.
4. Fireplace — plain brick opening 1.10 × 0.55 on a two-slab cast-stone plinth stepping out 0.20 m each side, 60 mm shelf slab at 1.20, two 0.20 m square lamp recesses, cap band at the ceiling; no mantel or surround. Rationale: plain brick openings with raised plinths stepping out both sides; every edge capped by a plain light band; small slot recesses as the chimney mass's only openings. Rules: R14, R08, R13.
5. Built-ins — window seat flush with the sill course, planter trough on the sill-wall base, stone-topped cabinet under the east pair. Rationale: window band above a low terrace wall carrying planter boxes; stone slabs cap every built-in. Rules: R11, R10, R08, R14.
6. Materials — tawny brick with only horizontal coursing expressed, cast-stone bands and slabs, oiled oak, leather over stuffing, leaded glass; no mirror, no gloss lacquer. Rules: R08, R09, R14.
7. Palette — warm: brick and pale stone dominant; oak, oxblood leather and copper accents; oatmeal textured wool and limewash neutrals. Rationale: dark brick bands between light strips; corpus accents of oak, leather, copper and stippled textured infill. Rules: R08, R15.
8. Proportions — every band and opening wider than high; leaves tall and narrow (0.84 × 1.80); gallery ceiling held low at 2.10 under 2.40. Rationale: horizontal bands many times wider than high, tall narrow main-storey lights, low stacked storeys, stepped layers. Rules: R01, R07, R14.
9. Lighting — leaded-glass box lanterns and wall lights, cove in the stepped head band, lamps in the breast recesses, square soffit downlights; all warm. Rules: R09, R14, R07, R13.
10. Elevation composition — fireplace wall asymmetrical as a whole (breast at x 1.35–3.15, door and dropped soffit at the north end, blank brick to the south); symmetry only within the fireplace face. Rule: R06.
11. Loose pieces (minor) — stone-slab table on stacked oak bands; 2.65 × 2.65 flatweave rug. Rule: R14.
12. Floor (minor) — quarter-sawn oak boards 140 mm, hardwax-oiled matt; satisfies the brief's named material and finish and avoids gloss. No rule cited.

Conflicts recorded (brief favoured on buildability and dimensions, rule on taste)
- R04 vs the rectangular 6.00 × 4.50 room: no prow bays possible; flat east wall with a two-leaf casement pair on the same module instead.
- R11 vs "continuous window band" and a single room with no balcony: windows with a 0.45 m sill course rather than floor-to-head glazed doors; outward-opening leaves, pier module and 75% glazing kept.
- R12 vs the required 0.80–1.00 m entry door: door provided as an interior door from the stair hall, flush in the brick with no architrave, surround, step or canopy.
- R03 / do-not (hearth never on an outside wall) vs "fireplace on one wall": the west wall is designated the central masonry core shared with the stair hall, not an exterior wall.

Rules at building scale with no room-scale counterpart (R02 two bars, R05 hipped roofs, R13 chimneys above roof beyond the slot motif, R15 top-storey panels beyond the textured upholstery) were not cited except where a genuine transfer is stated above. Everything the brief states has been met; nothing was dropped.
