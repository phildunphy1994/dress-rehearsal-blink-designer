# Hearth-End Living Room — Iteration Brief I1 (baseline arm)

Living room, 6.00 m × 4.50 m interior, 2.40 m ceiling. Geometry follows the brief convention: x runs east along the 6.00 m length, y runs north along the 4.50 m width, origin at the interior south-west corner. All dimensions in metres, 2 dp, and identical to spec.json.

## Layout

The room is organised as a single west–east sequence that ends at the fire.

- **Fireplace wall (west short wall).** A full-height honed limestone chimney breast (0.40 m deep, 1.90 m wide) is centred on the wall at y 2.25, with a raised slate hearth projecting 0.45 m. Either side, flush with the breast face, are oak alcove units: a 0.60 m high cupboard below and open shelves to the ceiling above. The whole 4.50 m wall reads as one plane of oak–stone–oak.
- **Window band (south long wall).** One continuous band of six lights runs from x 0.45 to x 4.35 (3.90 m = 65% of the wall), sill 0.85 m, head 2.20 m. It lights the seating group from the side so no seat faces glare.
- **Seating.** A U of three pieces around a 1.10 × 0.50 m oak coffee table, open to the fire: a three-seat sofa facing west (the principal seat, centred on the fireplace axis), a two-seat sofa under the window facing north, and a leather armchair on the north side facing south. Six seats in total. A flatweave rug runs under the front legs of all three.
- **Entry sequence (east end).** The entry door is at the south end of the east wall. You arrive in a 1.35 × 2.50 m vestibule under a soffit lowered to 2.20 m, with a full-height slatted oak screen to your left (west) and a coat cupboard ahead-right on the east wall. The vestibule releases northward onto an arrival landing at full ceiling height, with an oak console on the east wall. Door centre to nearest seat footprint is 1.84 m; the screen stands between the door and every seat.
- **Circulation.** From the landing a 1.10 m clear gallery corridor runs west along the bare north wall, passing the north end of the principal sofa, and turns south between the north alcove unit and the armchair to stand in front of the hearth. Minimum clear width on the whole route is 0.90 m (0.95 m in the vestibule beside the coat cupboard, 1.10–1.15 m along the corridor).

## Materials and finishes

- **Floor:** engineered oak, 190 mm wide plank, matt hardwax oil, laid along x (east–west) so the boards run toward the fire; continuous into the vestibule.
- **Walls:** clay plaster, matt trowelled finish, on north, south and east walls. No mirrored panels; no gloss or high-gloss lacquer on any wall surface.
- **Chimney breast:** honed limestone slabs on a blockwork breast, matt; blackened steel firebox frame around a sealed inset wood-burning firebox.
- **Hearth:** honed slate slab, 50 mm thick, matt.
- **Joinery (alcove units, screen, coat cupboard, console, mantel):** solid oak and oak veneer on MDF carcass, matt hardwax oil. Screen slats 40 × 40 mm at 80 mm centres.
- **Ceiling:** plaster, matt white emulsion; vestibule soffit in the same finish with a shadow-gap LED slot.
- **Upholstery:** wool bouclé (sofas), aniline waxed leather (armchair). Rug: undyed hand-loomed wool flatweave.
- **Metalwork:** blackened steel (firebox frame, lamp stems, coffee-table legs).
- Heating: underfloor heating assumed; no radiators under the window band.

## Decision log

1. **Fireplace on the west short wall, centred at y 2.25, full-height limestone between flush oak alcoves** — makes the hearth the terminus of the long axis so the entry sequence reads toward the fire. (major)
2. **Window band on the south long wall, x 0.45–4.35 = 65%, sill 0.85 / head 2.20** — exceeds the 60% minimum, lights the group from the side, and the sill clears the two-seat sofa's 0.80 m back. (major)
3. **Entry door at the south end of the east wall, hinged south, into a 1.35 × 2.50 m vestibule** — the farthest point from fire and seating; door centre is 1.84 m from the nearest seat (minimum 1.50). (major)
4. **Entry sequence of three named elements: slatted oak screen, coat cupboard, 2.20 m soffit** — compress-then-release; the screen stands between door and all seating and backs the principal sofa. (major)
5. **Seating as a U of 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 around the coffee table, open to the fire** — every seat sees the fire; three separate pieces keep a 0.90 m route possible in a 4.50 m wide room. (major)
6. **North long wall kept bare as a 1.10 m gallery corridor** — the only way to guarantee a 0.90 m clear route to the fireplace without cutting through the group; it becomes the picture wall. (major)
7. **Floor: engineered oak wide plank, matt hardwax oil, continuous into the vestibule** — warm, repairable, non-glossy; the threshold is marked by ceiling height, not floor. (major)
8. **Walls: matt clay plaster; no mirror panels, no gloss lacquer on any wall surface** — meets the brief prohibitions and keeps glare low beside a large window. (minor)
9. **Underfloor heating assumed, no radiators under the window band** — lets the two-seat sofa sit 0.20 m from the south wall and keeps the north corridor wide. (minor)
10. **Rug 2.40 × 2.50 m under the front legs of the seating** — binds three separate pieces into one group and softens acoustics. (minor)

Constraints not met: none. Note on interpretation: the circulation rule is read as a 0.90 m wide clear corridor centred on the path (0.45 m either side of the centreline); the designed route actually provides 0.95–1.15 m of clear floor along its length.
