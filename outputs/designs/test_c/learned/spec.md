# Brick Core and Glazed Band: Dining-Living Room

Brief: test_c (dining / living combined, 7.20 m x 4.50 m, ceiling 2.40 m). Arm: learned.

Geometry convention: origin at the interior south-west corner; x runs east along the 7.20 m length, y runs north across the 4.50 m width. South wall y = 0.00, north wall y = 4.50, west wall x = 0.00, east wall x = 7.20. Furniture positions give the south-west corner (x, y); L runs along x, D along y.

## Layout

The room is organised as two zones side by side on the long axis, living at the west end and dining at the east, with a thick brick hearth core projecting from the internal north wall at their junction. All service functions are gathered into a strip along that north wall: the entry door at its west end, a full-height oak coats cupboard beside it, the chimney breast with cast-stone plinth blocks stepping out on both sides, a long built-in sideboard, and the kitchen door at the east end. The principal space along the glazed south wall is left uninterrupted.

You enter by a plain flush oak door at the west end of the north wall into a compressed gallery 1.20 m deep under a 2.10 m dropped soffit (x 0.00-2.10, y 3.30-4.50). The coats cupboard and the chimney mass stand to your right (east); the room opens ahead to the south toward the glazed band and to the east along the promenade. The entry door, the hearth and the window groups share no centreline, so the composition is asymmetric overall while the fireplace face is symmetrical about its own centre.

The two zones are defined without any wall. The hearth core and its plinth blocks (x 1.80-3.90, projecting 0.90 m from the north wall) mark the junction; the gallery soffit, the living rug and the two furniture groups do the rest. No partition element spans the room width. Living zone polygon: x 0.00-3.75, full width. Dining zone polygon: x 3.75-7.20, full width.

The ceiling change required by the brief is a dropped plaster soffit at 2.10 m within the flat 2.40 m ceiling, in two places: the entry gallery soffit (x 0.00-2.10, y 3.30-4.50) and a 0.35 m deep eave soffit over the south glazed band (x 0.00-7.20, y 0.00-0.35). Each soffit edge carries a cast-stone fascia band, so the ceiling edge reads as a deep horizontal band oversailing the glazing heads. There is no level change; a floor step would steal height from a 2.40 m room.

The south wall is a continuous band of five 0.84 m glazed bays, floor to the 2.10 m head band, between 0.38 m brick piers on a 1.22 m module. The band stops one pier short of the corners with unequal corner piers (0.55 m west, 0.93 m east). Two bays are outward-swinging terrace leaves (S03 at x 2.99-3.83, facing the gap between sofa and dining chairs; S05 at x 0.55-1.39); the three bays behind the sofa and dining table are fixed lights. Both end walls are full-width groups of three lights between brick piers on a 0.90 m cast-stone sill course over a brick spandrel, each light a pair of 0.40 m tall narrow casements with a leaded straight-line ladder lattice. A built-in planter trough sits on the east sill.

The living group faces the fire across the promenade: a three-seat sofa at x 1.00-3.00 along the south side of the zone, an armchair beside a built-in cast-stone window seat under the west windows, an oak slab low table at the 0.40 m plinth datum, and a flatweave rug that stops at the promenade edge. The dining group is a 2.60 x 1.00 m oak slab table along the long axis with four tall-backed chairs a side (eight seats, no end chairs), 0.70 m from the east wall so one can pass round its end, and a linear oak-and-bronze pendant above.

Circulation: a straight promenade along y = 3.15 from the west wall to the east wall, clear between y 2.70 (window seat, armchair, low table, north dining chairs) and y 3.60 (hearth core and plinth blocks), and beyond that to the coats cupboard (3.90), sideboard (4.05) and entry door swing (3.60). Minimum clear width 0.90 m; actual minimum 1.00 m between the low table and the hearth blocks. A secondary route runs along the glazed band (y 0.00-0.90) west of x 3.90 and through the 1.00 m gap between sofa and dining chairs.

## Materials and finishes

- Walls: the north (hearth) wall, the south band piers, the end-wall piers and the spandrels below the 0.90 m sill course are bare tawny clay brick laid with raked horizontal joints and flush-tooled vertical joints, so only the horizontal coursing reads. Firebrick back and sides to the fire opening. No mirrored panels and no gloss finish on any wall surface.
- Cast stone: honed precast bands with plain square arrises and no mouldings: the continuous head band at 2.10-2.25 m (which caps the chimney breast), the fascia bands on both dropped soffits, the 0.90 m sill course on the end walls, the sideboard cap slab, the two hearth plinth blocks, the firebox floor and the window-seat slab.
- Ceiling and soffits: lime plaster, limewashed warm white, flat; no coffering or vault.
- Floor: wide-board European oak, 180 mm boards, matt hardwax oil, laid east-west.
- Joinery: solid European oak with matt hardwax oil throughout; flush doors with no architraves or surrounds; carcasses and furniture built from stacked slabs and square posts with square block feet; square oak pulls set out on a grid; no carved, turned or curved profile anywhere.
- Glazing: south band in plain thin oak frames with clear glass; end-wall casements in oak frames with a leaded straight-line lattice (ladder bars and a band of shallow lozenges at the head). All opening leaves and casements swing outward.
- Upholstery: oatmeal-brown textured wool boucle on sofa and armchair; oxblood full-grain aniline leather on dining chair pads and the window-seat squab.
- Metal: patinated bronze for pendant rods and underside, door and casement hardware, and the planter trough liner edge; zinc lining to the built-in planter trough on the east sill.
- Planting: built in only, a zinc-lined oak trough on the east window sill within the reveal; no freestanding pots.

## Decision log

1. Two zones side by side on the long axis with a brick hearth core projecting from the internal north wall at their junction; all service functions gathered in a strip along the north wall. The core marks the junction with both zones flowing past it, and the strip leaves the glazed principal space uninterrupted. [R01, R02, R03]
2. Entry by a plain flush door at the west end of the north wall into a 1.20 m deep, 2.10 m high gallery beside the coats cupboard and chimney mass; door, hearth and windows on different centrelines. The prescribed compressed vestibule beside a solid mass, keeping the composition asymmetric. [R12, R02, R06]
3. Ceiling change as two 2.10 m dropped soffits (entry gallery x 0.00-2.10 / y 3.30-4.50; south eave 0.35 m deep) with cast-stone fascias in a flat 2.40 m ceiling; no level change. A deep band oversailing the glazing heads and a low entry, without stealing floor height. [R05, R12, R01]
4. Zones defined without a wall by the hearth core and plinth blocks (x 1.80-3.90), the soffit, the rug and the furniture groups; nothing spans the room width. The designer's core sits between two rooms with passage past it. [R03, R02]
5. South wall as five 0.84 m floor-to-head glazed bays between 0.38 m brick piers on a 1.22 m module, corner piers 0.55 m west and 0.93 m east, two outward leaves and three fixed lights. The long outer wall is a glazed band of narrow leaves between short piers, stopping short of the corners and off the centreline. [R11, R07, R06]
6. End walls as three-light window groups between piers on a 0.90 m cast-stone sill course, each light a pair of 0.40 m casements with a leaded ladder lattice; planter trough built into the east sill. A plain rectangle gets a window group between piers, not a faked prow; lattice where glass is detailed; planting built in. [R04, R07, R09, R10]
7. Fireplace as a plain brick breast 1.50 m wide projecting 0.90 m to the ceiling, capped by the head band, 0.90 x 0.55 m opening raised 0.40 m, three square recesses, no mantel, cast-stone blocks stepping out both sides. Masonry core on the internal wall, plain capped slab with small recesses, plinths stepping out, every edge capped light. [R03, R13, R14, R08]
8. Eight dining seats at a 2.60 x 1.00 m oak slab table along the long axis, four a side, no end chairs; 0.70 m to the east wall and behind the south chairs, 1.35 m behind the north chairs. End chairs would need more length than the room has; the 4+4 slab reads long and low and clears the promenade. [R01, R14]
9. Living group facing the fire across the promenade, with a cast-stone window seat under the west windows, an oak slab low table at the 0.40 m datum and a rug stopping at the promenade. Passage past the core is the designer's move; the window seat is a plinth slab under the sill course. [R03, R14, R01]
10. Built-ins and furniture from stacked oak slabs and square posts with square pulls on a grid; sideboard capped in cast stone at the 0.90 m datum; no curved profile. Stacked rectangular layers, light caps on built-ins, shared datums. [R14, R08, R01]
11. Palette of tawny brick, pale cast stone, oiled oak, warm limewash, oxblood leather, oatmeal wool and patinated bronze; no mirror, no gloss on walls. Dark brick bands between light strips with warm oak, leather and stippled upholstery. [R08, R14]
12. Floor: wide-board oak, matt hardwax oil, laid east-west. One light warm floor keeps the low room bright and the boards run the long way. [R01]
13. Proportions held to four datums (0.40, 0.90, 2.10, 2.40); openings wider than high; dining chairs tall and narrow (1.15 m on 0.42 m), living seating low and wide. Continuous bands read long and low; the designer's chair and opening proportions. [R01, R14]
14. Lighting as continuous lines in the ceiling steps and on the head band, a linear pendant slab, grazing downlights on the breast, block-base table lamps. Light laid along the horizontal bands with fittings as stacked slabs. [R01, R05, R14]
15. Circulation as a straight promenade along y 3.15 from end wall to end wall, 0.90 m minimum clear (1.00 m actual), passing the core and the table's north side; secondary route along the glazed band. Both zones flow past the core on one line linking entry, terrace and kitchen. [R03]
16. Minor: entry door hinged west swinging in with nothing in its arc; kitchen door and terrace leaves swing out. Keeps every swing clear. [no rule]

Conflicts recorded (rule vs brief):
- R03 vs the brief's hearth WALL as principal elevation and the 0.90 m end-to-end path: a freestanding core on the long axis would leave no hearth wall to draw and block the dining group, so the core is attached to the internal north wall with passage past it on the room side only. Brief favoured on buildability.
- R07 vs the stated 7.20 x 4.50 m dimensions: the 1.22 m module does not divide them; the remainder is taken up in unequal corner piers. Brief dimensions untouched.
- R04 vs the plain rectangular room: no prow bay; end walls as window groups between piers (the rule's own fallback).
- R11 vs the 8-seat dining group inside the room: the three bays behind the sofa and table are fixed lights, the two leaves that open face clear floor; the band stays continuous.

Nothing in the brief has been dropped: two named zones, a located ceiling change, eight dining seats, a hearth, a 0.90 m end-to-end path, two doors of 0.90 m and twelve windows, a named floor finish, no mirror or gloss on walls, no furniture in a door swing, no overlapping footprints (rug and pendant flagged as allowed overlaps).
