# Living room: hearth wall, ribbon window and oak entry screen

Brief test_a, arm baseline. Room 5.50 m (x, east) x 4.20 m (y, north), ceiling 2.30 m. Origin at the interior south-west corner. All dimensions in metres.

## Layout

A low, long room organised along its length. You enter through a 0.90 m door at the south end of the west wall into a 1.00 m wide vestibule formed by a floor-to-soffit oak entry screen set 1.00 m off the west wall. The screen is the named entry element: its south 1.20 m is a closed coat cupboard with shoe drawers facing the vestibule, its north 1.50 m is open oak slats that let the north window light filter through. The ceiling over the vestibule and screen drops to a 2.15 m limewashed soffit, then lifts to the full 2.30 m as you pass the end of the screen.

Beyond the screen the living zone runs 3.60 m to the east wall. A 2.20 m three-seat sofa stands with its back to the screen, facing east to the fire. A 1.80 m built-in window seat for two sits under the ribbon window on the north wall and forms the north arm of an L around a low oak coffee table on a flatweave rug. To the west of the window seat, a matching low oak ledge unit with open shelving continues the same 0.42 m line to the screen.

The east wall is the fireplace wall: a continuous 0.45 m deep honed basalt plinth, 0.42 m high, runs the full 4.20 m width, with a 1.60 m wide full-height basalt chimney breast at its centre. The fire opening sits on the plinth datum. An oak-lined log niche is cut into the north plinth.

Circulation runs along the south side of the room, 1.00 m clear between the south wall and the sofa and table, then turns north in front of the hearth, 1.35 m clear between the table and the plinth. The door centre is 1.62 m from the nearest seating footprint (the sofa's south-west corner), and the screen stands between the door and all seating.

## Materials and finishes

- Floor: smoked European oak engineered boards, 220 mm wide, matt hardwax oil, laid east-west; oak flush skirting 60 mm on the north, south and west walls (the basalt plinth replaces the skirting on the east wall).
- Walls: hand-trowelled lime plaster, matt (no mirrored panels, no gloss lacquer anywhere).
- Ceiling and soffit: limewash on skimmed plasterboard, matt.
- Fireplace wall: 20 mm honed grey basalt slabs on blockwork for the plinths and chimney breast; blackened steel surround to a sealed, glass-fronted insert flued within the east wall; oak-lined log niche.
- Joinery (entry screen, ledge unit, window seat): European oak veneer on birch ply carcasses, solid oak slats 40 x 40 mm at 40 mm gaps, solid oak lippings, matt hardwax oil; push-to-open drawers.
- Window: slim thermally broken frames, blackened finish; fixed ribbon glazing in five equal lights; recessed blind pocket above the head.
- Door: flush oak-veneered leaf, matt oil, blackened steel lever handle.
- Upholstery: moss green wool boucle on the sofa; ochre heavy linen on the window seat cushions.
- Rug: undyed wool flatweave.

## Decision log

1. Fireplace on the east short wall as a full-width basalt plinth with a central full-height chimney breast. Rationale: the long axis ends on the hearth; both long walls stay free for the ribbon window and the sofa back; the principal elevation is one symmetric low composition. (major)
2. Ribbon window on the north wall, 3.75 m (68%), sill 1.00, head 2.00. Rationale: even north light without glare on fire-facing seating; the 2.00 head leaves a 0.30 pocket for blind and concealed light under a 2.30 ceiling. (major)
3. Entry door at the south-west corner of the west wall with a 0.45 deep oak screen and coat store forming a 1.00 wide vestibule. Rationale: the screen is the named entry element between door and seating; door centre 1.62 from the nearest seating footprint. (major)
4. Dropped soffit at 2.15 over vestibule and screen. Rationale: compression at the threshold makes the 2.30 living ceiling feel taller and houses the entry downlights. (minor)
5. Five seats as a three-seat sofa facing the fire plus a 1.80 window seat for two, in an L around a low table. Rationale: no second freestanding sofa, so the 1.00 south corridor stays clear; the window seat uses the depth under the ribbon window. (major)
6. One continuous 0.42 datum for plinths, window seat and ledge unit, with the fire opening sitting on it. Rationale: a single low line round two walls stretches the room and keeps everything below eye level. (major)
7. Circulation along the south side (1.00 clear) turning north in front of the hearth. Rationale: reaches the sofa and the fire without crossing the seating group or the rug. (major)
8. No pendants; concealed linear light at window head, plinth shadow gaps and under the ledge, plus one floor and one table lamp. Rationale: hanging fittings read as obstacles under 2.30; grazing light along the long walls emphasises length. (minor)
9. All wall and joinery surfaces matt: lime plaster, honed basalt, oiled oak. Rationale: meets the no-mirror, no-gloss constraint and avoids busy reflections. (minor)
10. Smoked oak wide boards laid east-west. Rationale: boards running the long way reinforce the long, low proportion and link the three zones as one floor. (minor)

Constraints check: fireplace wall (east) present; ribbon window on a long wall at 68%; seating 5; door centre 1.62 m from seating with a named entry screen and ceiling change between; entry door 0.90 wide; floor finish named with material and finish; no mirrored panels or gloss lacquer; path 0.90 minimum met at 1.00 and 1.35; no furniture in the door swing; all footprints inside the room and non-overlapping (rug excepted, flagged). Nothing in the brief could not be met.
