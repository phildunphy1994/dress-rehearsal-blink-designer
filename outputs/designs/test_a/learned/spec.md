# Test Brief A — Living room, low and long (arm: learned)

Title: Low living bar with a brick hearth core and a south ribbon

## Layout (in words)

The 5.50 × 4.20 m room is organised as two parallel east–west bars. Along the north wall runs a 1.15 m service strip: at its west end a low oak log store, then a thick brick chimney breast standing on a raised stone-capped plinth (centred at x 1.90 m, west of the room's centreline), then a full-height oak coat press, and at the east end the entry vestibule. South of this strip the principal sitting bar, 5.50 × 3.05 m, lies along the glazed south wall.

The entry is a flush oak door at the east end of the north wall. It opens inward into a compressed vestibule, 1.80 × 1.25 m under a 2.05 m soffit, bounded on the west by the solid end of the coat press and on the south by a slatted oak screen; the only way on is a 0.95 m gap along the east wall, after which the room opens westward with the window ribbon on the left and the hearth ahead-right. The door's centre is 2.22 m from the nearest seat. Named entry elements: the vestibule (ceiling change to 2.05 m), the slatted screen, the coat press.

The south wall carries a ribbon of four tall casement leaves between short brick piers on a 1.22 m module, set on a continuous 0.80 m cast-stone sill course with their heads at the 2.05 m soffit; the band stops one pier short of each corner (0.38 m east, 0.56 m west). The west end wall is expressed as a full-width group of three casements between piers rather than a faked prow; the sill course wraps the corner. The east wall is plain plaster.

Seating for five: a built-in three-seat window bench under leaves W2–W3 (its seat slab on the 0.45 m plinth datum), and two oak-and-leather armchairs facing each other across a low oak table, all south of a 0.90 m corridor that runs along the front of the core from the vestibule to the hearth. A copper-lined planter trough sits on the sill course under the eastmost leaf.

Three horizontal datums run uninterrupted: 0.45 m (one cast-stone slab over log store and hearth plinth; window-bench seat), 0.80 m (sill course, south and west), 2.05 m (door head, window heads, soffit underside, breast cap). The ceiling is flat at 2.30 m inside a continuous 0.45 m-wide dropped soffit band that oversails every opening like an eave. The composition is asymmetric overall: door at x 4.80 m, hearth at x 1.90 m, window band centred at x 2.84 m.

## Materials and finishes

- Floor: European oak wide boards 180 mm, hardwax-oiled matt, laid east–west along the long axis.
- Masonry: tawny clay brick with struck horizontal joints only (no vertical joints expressed), sealed matt — chimney breast, hearth plinth, window piers, the low walls below the sill course on the south and west walls, the planter trough. Every masonry edge is capped by honed pale cast stone: the 0.80 m sill course (projecting 0.30 m as a shelf), the 0.45 m plinth slab, the breast cap, the screen plinth strip, the planter rim.
- Walls (north, east) and all ceilings/soffits: lime plaster, matt. No mirrored panels; no high-gloss lacquer on any wall surface.
- Joinery: European oak, hardwax-oiled satin, built from stacked slabs and square posts — flush slab doors with square oak pulls on a grid, oversailing top slabs with thinner secondary rails, square block feet, stepped cap finials on chair posts; no curved mouldings, no carving or turning. Windows: thin oak frames and mullions.
- Glazing: straight-line leaded lattice in every casement — vertical ladder bars at 140 mm, chevron head, a band of three shallow lozenges (about 3:1) above the sill.
- Upholstery: oxblood vegetable-tanned leather over wool stuffing (seats and chair backs); stippled textured wool for the bench back bolster.
- Metal: natural unlacquered copper for pendant shades and planter liner. Firebox lined in firebrick.

Conflicts recorded (rule vs brief)
- R11 vs Must 2 (ribbon windows): R11 wants floor-to-head glazed door leaves; the brief names windows, so tall casements on a 0.80 m sill course (R07) are used. Brief favoured on element type; rule kept on module, piers, outward swing and corner stop.
- R01 vs the stated room size 5.50 × 4.20 × 2.30: the 1.31:1 plan cannot approach the corpus's long bar; dimensions kept exactly and the long-low reading made with the service strip, soffit band, continuous datums and east–west boards.
- R03 vs Must 1 (fireplace on one wall of a single room): the north wall is chosen because it reads internal (the entry arrives through it); the hearth is a projecting mass the room flows past on its south face and west flank. A true open passage past the flank into an adjoining room is not available in a one-room brief, so the "passage past the core" is the 0.90 m corridor along its front.

Nothing in the brief was dropped: fireplace wall (north), ribbon ≥ 60 % (65 % glass / 83 % band on the south wall), seating 5, entry door 0.90 m with the door centre 2.22 m from seating and three named entry elements, floor finish named, no mirrored panels, no high-gloss wall finish, ≥ 0.90 m circulation to bench and hearth, no footprint in the door swing, no overlapping footprints, everything inside the room.
