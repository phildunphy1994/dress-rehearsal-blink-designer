# Dining room — iteration_i2, learned arm

Banded dining room: brick plinth bench, oak head band and a glazed south ribbon.

## Layout

The room is one bay of a long east–west living-dining bar, and it is organised along that long axis. The kitchen is to the west, the living room to the east, and the whole south wall faces a balcony.

The kitchen door sits at the south corner of the west short wall. From it a single straight service aisle, 1.65 m clear, runs the full length of the room along the glazed south wall to a leafless opening at the south end of the east wall, which leads past the blank brick face of the chimney core into the living room. Both connections therefore feed one straight line of movement, and the two rooms read as one space with no expressed doorway between them.

North of the aisle the dining table (2.40 × 0.90 m) lies on the long axis, centred in the room with 0.70 m beyond each end. Three loose chairs stand on the aisle side and one at each end. Behind the table the entire north wall is one continuous 0.50 m deep built-in band: a short, taller sideboard block at the kitchen (west) end, and a long low banquette that runs from the sideboard to the east wall. The banquette gives three dining seats at the table; its eastern 1.20 m runs on to the core wall as a side seat beside the living-room passage. The sideboard is served from the strip west of the table, nearest the kitchen door.

The south wall is a ribbon of three outward-swinging glazed door leaves between narrow brick piers on a 1.22 m module, set toward the east end. At the kitchen corner the wall is left solid for 0.76 m so the inward-opening kitchen door can lie back against brick without striking a glazed leaf. The glazed leaves open outward, so nothing in the room sits in any door swing.

Everything is banded horizontally: a brick plinth to 0.38 m, a light cast-stone line at 0.90–0.96 m (sideboard top, bench rail, string course on the end walls), a stippled plaster frieze at 1.90–2.20 m, and an oak stepped head band at 2.20–2.40 m that is also the head of every opening. The ceiling is flat at 2.40 m with no beams or drops. No fireplace is built: the hearth belongs in the central core, which is implied beyond the east wall.

## Materials and finishes

- Floor: fired clay quarry tile 150 × 150 × 20 mm, tawny red, laid in running bond with the courses running east–west along the long axis, matt penetrating sealer.
- Walls: tawny facing brick on all four walls from floor to 1.90 m, bed joints raked, perpends flush and colour-matched so each wall reads as continuous horizontal courses with no vertical joints expressed. No mirrored panels and no gloss lacquer anywhere.
- Cast stone (honed, plain square edges, light limestone colour): sideboard top slab 0.06 m, shelf slab 0.06 m, bench seat slab 0.05 m, and a flush string course 0.90–0.96 m on the west and east walls so the light line runs round the room.
- Oak (solid European oak, hardwax oil, matt): stepped head band 2.20–2.40 m with a coved soffit on all four walls and over every opening; plain frames to the kitchen door, glazed leaves and east passage; frieze frames; two flush sideboard doors; bench top rail (three-layer stepped cap); table; chairs; pendant frames. All profiles are stacked rectangular layers; no carving, turning or applied mouldings.
- Frieze 1.90–2.20 m: stippled (textured float) lime plaster panels in plain oak frames on the north, east and west walls, interrupted only by openings. Ceiling: smooth lime plaster, limewashed, flat at 2.40 m.
- Glazing: oak-framed outward-swinging glazed door leaves with straight-line leaded lattice — tall narrow border lights, a field of shallow lozenges about three times as wide as high, a stack of small square lights in the top tier.
- Upholstery: saddle leather over hair-and-wool stuffing for the bench cushion; stipple-textured umber wool for the bench back pad and chair seat pads.
- Metal: brushed, waxed copper for the pendant liners and stems and the kitchen door pull.
- Doors: plain flush oak kitchen door leaf, no architrave, no threshold step; the living-room connection is a leafless opening.

## Decision log

1. Organise the room on its long east–west axis — table on the axis, built-ins consolidated into one low band on the north wall, aisle along the glazed south wall, kitchen west and living room east — so the room reads as one bay of a long living-dining bar, every element a horizontal band under a low ceiling. [R01, R03, R11]
2. Kitchen door 0.90 × 2.20 at the south corner of the west wall, inward-opening, hinged on the south jamb, plain flush oak leaf with no architrave, step or surround — doors few, flush and plain, with the swing kept in the aisle. [R12, R14]
3. Living-room connection as a leafless 1.22 × 2.20 opening at the south end of the east wall beside the blank brick core face — the rooms flow past the core as one space with no expressed doorway. [R03, R12]
4. South wall as a ribbon of three outward-swinging glazed leaves 0.84 × 2.20 between 0.38 brick piers on a 1.22 module, continuous head at 2.20, offset east with a 0.76 solid return at the kitchen corner — the dining room's south wall almost entirely glazed doors onto a balcony; stopped short of the corner so the kitchen door can open against brick, which also keeps the wall asymmetrical. [R11, R07, R01]
5. Straight-line leaded lattice in every glazed leaf (border lights, shallow lozenges three times as wide as high, small squares in the top tier) — the geometric leaded pattern used where glazing is detailed; mixed evidence accepted. [R09]
6. Banquette as a low brick wall: coursed plinth to 0.38, cast-stone seat slab, leather cushion to 0.49, wool back pad, oak stepped rail at 0.90–0.96, running from the sideboard to the east wall with three seats at the table — a coping slab on a brick plinth, profiles built from stacked rectangular layers, no carving or mantel-like ornament. [R10, R08, R14]
7. Sideboard at the kitchen end of the north wall with the same plinth, two flush oak doors, cast-stone top at 0.90–0.96 and a cantilevered stone shelf slab 1.20 × 0.25 × 0.06 at 1.46–1.52 — the service piece offset to one end makes the built-in wall an asymmetrical stepped composition rather than a centred one; the shelf a plain slab much wider than high. [R02, R06, R14]
8. No fireplace or hearth in the room — the hearth belongs in the central core between living and dining rooms and never on an outside wall; here every wall is a perimeter wall, so the core is implied beyond the east wall. [R03]
9. Materials: tawny brick with raked bed joints and flush colour-matched perpends; plain honed cast-stone caps, slabs and string course; oiled oak joinery; leather and stippled wool; stippled lime-plaster frieze panels in oak frames at 1.90–2.20; tawny-red quarry tile floor in east–west running bond; no mirrors, no gloss lacquer — brick as horizontal courses only, every edge capped by a plain light band, textured panels in a framed top-tier band, floor coursing continuing the banding. [R08, R14, R15]
10. Palette: tawny brick and red tile dominant, light cast stone as the neutral strip, oak, leather, umber wool and copper as warm accents, warm putty plaster above — dark warm brick bands between plain light strips with the warm accents of tile, copper and oak. [R08]
11. Proportions: table 2.40 × 0.90 × 0.74 (2.67:1, low); chairs 0.42 wide × 1.25 high (about 3:1); glazed leaves 0.84 × 2.20 on a 1.22 module split 0.84/0.38; bench 3.60 long, seat 0.49; flat 2.40 ceiling — long low horizontals, tall narrow lights, the measured leaf-and-pier module. [R01, R07]
12. Lighting: continuous concealed LED in the coved soffit of the head band on all four walls; three stepped oak box pendants with leaded glass and copper liners over the table at 1.60; strip under the shelf slab — the stepped head band becomes the light source and keeps the ceiling plain; fittings built from stacked rectangular layers and leaded glass, not turned or ornamented. [R14, R07, R09]
13. Seating count 3 + 3 + 2 = 8 — fixed seating on the wall side, loose chairs on the aisle side so the aisle stays clear. (minor, no rule)
14. Circulation: one straight 1.65 m aisle from the kitchen door to the east passage, 0.70 m end strips, glazed leaves swinging out, nothing in the kitchen-door swing — exceeds the required 0.90 m clear path. (minor, no rule)
15. Flush floor throughout with no level change or raised threshold at any opening — doors at floor level with no step or surround. [R12]
